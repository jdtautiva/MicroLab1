Activity Instructions

  1. 1
    Form your team — Work in groups of 3. Each group will select a different firm from the 10 available. Each firm has unique parameters — cross-group copying will not help.
  2. 2
    Complete the cost table by hand — Each round, TC and VC are pre-filled. Your group must calculate MC, AVC, and ATC using the formulas and enter each value. The table self-corrects in real time: green = correct, red = revise.
  3. 3
    Analyze the chart — Once all cells are correct, the chart appears automatically showing ATC, MC, the market price line, and the long-run equilibrium point. Use it to support your production decision.
  4. 4
    Make your production decision — Apply the relevant economic condition for each round and enter the optimal quantity. If your answer is incorrect, you will receive an economic hint — the simulator never reveals the answer directly.
  5. 5
    Write your group conclusion — After each correct decision, your group must write a brief conclusion before advancing. It will be included in your final report.
  6. 6
    Export your PDF report — After completing all 4 rounds, download a full report with results, conclusions, performance feedback, and plenary discussion questions.

Economic cases covered
1
Competitive Market Equilibrium
The firm is a price-taker. The goal is to find the quantity that maximizes profit.
P = MC → q* = (P − a) / (2b)
2
Price Drop & Shutdown Condition
When price falls, the firm decides whether to adjust output or shut down temporarily to minimize losses.
Operate if P ≥ AVC min (= a)
3
Input Cost Shock & Shutdown Point
A rise in variable costs shifts MC and AVC upward, reducing q* or forcing shutdown.
MC_adj = (a + 2bq)·m; shutdown if P < a·m
4
Short-Run Supply Curve
The SR supply curve is the segment of MC above AVC minimum, evaluated at different price levels.
S(P): q* for each P ≥ AVC min

Quadratic Cost Structure

The following cost functions are exclusive to this activity and have been designed for pedagogical purposes. In real business contexts, cost functions may vary significantly depending on industry, production technology, and market structure.

Cost functions — TC(q) = FC + a·q + b·q²
VC(q) = a·q + b·q²   ·   MC(q) = a + 2b·q
AVC(q) = a + b·q   ·   ATC(q) = FC/q + a + b·q
LR equilibrium: q_eq = √(FC / b)  →  min price = ATC(q_eq)

▶ All costs and prices are expressed in USD  ·  q represents lots of 100 bread units

Each round you will complete the cost table by hand. Once all cells are verified, the system will automatically display the chart with MC, ATC, and the equilibrium point marked.
